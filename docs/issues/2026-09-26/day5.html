Kevin Marino CabreraUS ambassador to PanamaPanama City Aviation is the hardest human industry to decarbonise, and will take decades of technological transformation. Contrail manipulation is a relatively cheap and easy way to blunt the warming effects of aircrafts’ contrails (”Bluer cooler skies”, August 22nd). So far, research has focused on contrail avoidance. But there is a place for contrail creation. Since contrails are generally short-lived, it can be beneficial to purposely create them during the day (which makes the Earth more reflective of the Sun, a cooling effect) and avoid them at night, where they reflect infrared radiation back to Earth, warming it. Accurate atmospheric modelling is required to make such a strategy effective, and such models continue to improve. Dr Bruce SawhillRunway EnergeticsSanta Cruz, California Aircraft contrails may warm the planet more than the carbon dioxide from aircraft engines, you report. This is true if air traffic keeps growing after contrail warming is reduced by two thirds, no longer exceeding the direct carbon warming. In other words, the case for rerouting is strongest when aviation continues to grow. Geography also limits re-routing’s effectiveness where aviation is growing fastest. Area for area, contrails warm the atmosphere over Europe more than ten times as intensely as over China and East Asia, where aircraft cruise lower and the subtropical air is drier. In these regions, almost all long-lived contrails form inside pre-existing ice clouds, where the added warming is small and may even turn to cooling. Re-routing aircraft is cheap and worth trying, but it’s a one-shot effect. The warming from aviation’s carbon emissions, on the other hand, will continue to grow as long as jet fuel is burned. The future of digital finance rests on public money, not private Central banks will be needed to maintain trust and ensure interoperability, argues Piero Cipollone Sep 24th 2026 IN 2008 an obscure manifesto introduced a new vision of money that promised to make central banks obsolete. The techno-futurist ambitions of bitcoin’s pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, captured the public imagination—and sparked the wild booms and busts of unbacked crypto assets. More recently, the focus has been shifting towards the use of crypto’s underlying technologies—tokenisation and distributed ledgers—in mainstream finance. These innovations enable assets such as bonds, shares or deposits to be represented, recorded and transferred as digital packets of data, or tokens. Combining distributed ledgers with tokenisation promises a more efficient way to offer financial services, around the clock and with far fewer intermediaries than today.
