A bigger factor is that the world’s supply of crude currently outpaces demand—not because production has surged, but because idled refineries are consuming less. Chinese refineries are processing 3m b/d fewer than in February. China is usually a huge exporter of petrol and diesel, but the government prohibited state-owned refiners from selling abroad during much of the war. That ban was lifted in July, as long as refiners’ stocks did not drop below the pre-war levels of late February. China’s big oil firms have tentatively increased their collective export target to roughly two-thirds of what they were selling a year ago.
