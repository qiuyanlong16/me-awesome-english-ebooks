Christianity had a go, as did various emperors, to little avail. Esperanto never quite caught on. Football and the Eurovision Song Contest bring the place together for a few nights each year. Perhaps Europe’s common culture is found not in stadiums or concert halls, but oozing from its government offices: battling red tape is the thread that binds the continent together. While Europeans disagree on politics, cuisine and religion, all are united in living under the administrator’s ink-stained thumb. From Dublin to Athens there is a reassuring familiarity to being informed that the correct form is unavailable, that another office must be consulted (preferably by fax), then that the deadline expired yesterday. The European Union in Brussels, the spiritual home of the paperwork-industrial complex, is a fitting

culmination of attempts to forge ever-closer union. Where mere emperors failed, the rubber-stamping Eurocrat has triumphed. All polities spawn paperwork.
