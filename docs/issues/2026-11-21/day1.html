Nov 19th 2026 Donald Trump attended the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation summit in Shenzhen. He was given a lavish welcome by Xi Jinping, which Mr Trump said “was great respect, and I appreciate that”. He held one-on-one talks with Mr Xi and also with Vladimir Putin before the three men met for trilateral talks, the first ever formal discussions between the leaders of America, Russia and China. Before the APEC summit Mr Trump visited the Association of South-East Asian Nations forum in Manila, where he held a meeting with the leaders of Australia, India and Japan, countries that with America form the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue, a forum co-operating on defence and economics to counter China’s growing power in the region. Before the APEC summit two suspected Russian military aircraft entered Japanese air space near the island of Okinoshima, which is located 80km (50 miles) north of the city of Fukuoka. Japan lodged a formal protest with Moscow, describing the incident as “a serious infringement of Japan’s

sovereignty”. Taiwan also reported that it had spotted two Russian planes close to its east coast on two separate occasions. The International Court of Justice in The Hague ruled that Myanmar had committed genocide against the Rohingya people in 2017. The court found that Myanmar’s security forces “perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence as well as acts of extreme violence against children and attacked fleeing Rohingya” in their attempt to drive them out of Rakhine state. International courts have made other determinations of genocide in Rwanda, Srebrenica and Cambodia, but this is the first time a state has been found to have carried out such crimes. Ukraine mounted another big drone attack on Russian infrastructure and partly destroyed the Kerch bridge, which links Russia to Crimea. The bridge was built by Russia following its annexation of Crimea in 2014. Meanwhile, Jared Kushner, one of Mr Trump’s envoys, said progress was being made in negotiations to end the war. America wants Ukraine’s European allies to be involved in the next stage of talks, but the Kremlin dismissed suggestions that Russia had agreed to their participation. Russia had said earlier the EU needed to choose a representative if it wanted to attend negotiations. Eight people were arrested in Estonia for an attempted attack on a defence company that develops technology for drones which are supplied to Ukraine. The government said the attack was directed by Russian military intelligence, and in response threatened to shut Estonia’s border with Russia from December 1st. In Turkey the authorities seized control of the last pro-opposition television station. Prosecutors allege that the owner of Sozcu TV has links to the Gulen movement, which the government blames for an attempted coup in 2016. Lawyers for the station claimed that earlier court cases found no evidence of wrongdoing. Sozcu TV has been reporting on the investigations into a financial scandal that has wiped out the savings of nearly half a million people. An independent review by Alan Milburn, a former government minister, on working opportunities for young people in Britain proposed a ten-year plan to tackle persistently high disengagement from the workforce. Those not in

education, employment or training (NEETs) make up 13% of 16- to 24-year- olds. The review suggests a path to reduce this to 5% by 2036. The government welcomed the report, but many businesses say that recent regulatory changes and tax rises make it too costly to employ young workers. Andy Burnham declared that Britain lost more than it gained from leaving the European Union, as the prime minister prepared to meet EU leaders for a summit that will discuss closer co-operation on a range of issues. The British are focusing on agreements to reduce red tape in the food-and-drink trade and to align carbon-emissions policy. Meanwhile, it was announced that Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands will deport illegal migrants to a “return hub” in Rwanda starting next year. EU legislation now allows countries to send migrants to facilities outside Europe. The pilot scheme in Rwanda would apply to migrants who have exhausted all their legal options and are unable to return to their country of origin. Human-rights groups criticised the deal, saying it would finance and legitimise a government responsible for serious abuses. Members of the Israeli Knesset elected Matan Kahana as speaker. Mr Kahana is a member of the centre-right party led by Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form a government. The new speaker takes hardline positions on Israeli settlements and any possibility of a future Palestinian state, but both Jewish and Arab opposition parties voted for him. America attacked Qeshm Island off Iran’s coast in response to Iranian aggression. Bahrain and Kuwait intercepted several aerial attacks; Iran said it was targeting American air-defence systems and the Ali al-Salem air base in Kuwait, where America maintains a presence. Iran made several claims of tankers hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Pro-government forces in Yemen announced their withdrawal from territory overlooking Bab al-Mandab, a vital waterway into the Red Sea. The forces are backed by Saudi Arabia and are shifting their troops to reinforce positions around Aden, the interim capital, as fighting intensifies against the Houthis, Iranian-backed rebels. The Houthis have ramped up attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure.

South Sudan again postponed elections, from December 22nd to February 11th, ostensibly to give authorities more time to register voters. Armed conflict has intensified since the collapse of a power-sharing deal last year between Salva Kiir, the president, and Riek Machar, his main rival and former vice-president, who has been charged with treason and put under house arrest. A legitimate election looks far off. Pete Hegseth, America’s secretary of war, visited Venezuela, where he announced a pact to co-operate on military and security matters, covering training and intelligence. Mr Hegseth warned drug traffickers that “Your time is up. We will hunt you down.” At least 240 people have been killed in America’s controversial policy of conducting strikes on suspected drug- running boats. A journalist who investigated possible links between drug-traffickers and politicians in Costa Rica was shot dead. It is the first known assassination in 25 years of a journalist in Costa Rica, which is relatively peaceful compared with its neighbours, though drug violence is on the rise. In Haiti Martine Moïse, the widow of Jovenel Moïse, the former president who was assassinated in 2021, registered as a candidate in the presidential election due to be held next July. The election has been long delayed, and could be again if security forces cannot control gang violence. Haiti’s security minister credited the deployment of troops from El Salvador with helping to curb the trouble, before meeting the Salvadoran president, Nayib Bukele, for talks about extending the deployment.

Two weeks after the midterm elections, Mary Peltola was declared the winner in Alaska’s Senate race. Her victory means the Democrats will hold 56 seats in the Senate when it convenes in January, to the Republicans’ 44. After spending close to $500m on Republican campaigns in the midterms Elon Musk now says he feels “pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seeing much for it”.
